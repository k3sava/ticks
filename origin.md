Each one isn't just a technical breakthrough. It's a constraint dissolved.

Ilya's moment: you don't need to hand-engineer features if you scale compute + data. The constraint was human legibility of representation.
Satoshi's moment: you don't need trusted institutions if you replace trust with math + incentives. The constraint was who verifies truth.
Zuckerberg's moment: the Like button made approval legible at scale — suddenly human social behavior had a signal that could be aggregated, ranked, fed back. The constraint was quantifying connection.
The internet's moment: information doesn't need to move through gatekeepers if you make the pipes open and dumb. The constraint was distribution as a chokepoint.
The click isn't always invention. Often it's reframing what the actual bottleneck is — and realizing the bottleneck is softer than everyone assumed.

What's interesting is these moments usually look obvious in retrospect and absurd at the time. Satoshi's whitepaper was largely ignored. The Like button was almost cut. Ilya's scaling bet was against the consensus of the field for years.

Which makes you wonder — what are the constraints right now that feel structural but are actually just assumed? The ones where someone is already sitting on the click, and we'll read about it in ten years and think of course.
